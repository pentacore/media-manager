# Jobs

## Queued jobs
Implement `ShouldQueue` with the single `Queueable` trait, declare `tries`/`timeout`/`backoff` as typed public properties (except on a `final` job, where Rector's `*PropertyTo*AttributeRector` rules (Laravel attribute set) require `#[Tries(n)]`, `#[Timeout(n)]` and `#[Backoff(n)]` (`Illuminate\Queue\Attributes\*`) instead — the CI Rector dry run fails on the property form there), and add `ShouldBeUnique` with a `uniqueId()` for reconcile and agent jobs (timeout below queue `retry_after`). Route a job to its lane with `#[Queue(QueueLane::X)]` (`Illuminate\Queue\Attributes\Queue`): `Actions` for action execution (including a delayed wake-up that hands off to `ExecuteActionRequest`, such as `ExecuteDebouncedLibraryScan`), `Webhooks` for webhook processing, `Ai` for any job that calls a model provider (agents, embeddings, titles, price refresh), `Maintenance` for long, low-priority housekeeping — a job whose own timeout runs multi-minute (retention/reconcile/sync sweeps such as `ClearSeerrRequests`, `ReconcileSearchIndex`, `SyncAnimeMappingJob`) that would otherwise pin the general worker behind webhooks and approved actions; every other job carries no attribute and stays on `default`. `Ai` and `Maintenance` share the dedicated `queue-ai` worker (ai drained first), so neither delays an approved action or an inbound webhook. Never call `onQueue()` at a dispatch site; the only exception is a job parameterised by lane (`RecordQueueHeartbeat`), which calls `$this->onQueue()` in its constructor. Keep every job timeout below its worker's `--timeout` (300s) and the redis `retry_after` (330s). Put throttling/concurrency limits in a job middleware class under `app/Jobs/Middleware`. Carry payload snapshots, not models, when the row may be trimmed; set `$deleteWhenMissingModels = true` when a model may vanish.
